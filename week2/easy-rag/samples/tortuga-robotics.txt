TORTUGA ROBOTICS — COMPANY FACT SHEET
Internal document, revision 7. Last updated 12 March 2026.

ABOUT
Tortuga Robotics builds slow, careful warehouse robots. The company was founded
in Manresa, Catalonia, in November 2019 by Núria Balaguer and Ivan Prohaska,
after both left a larger automation firm over a disagreement about safety
margins. The name comes from the founders' rule: a robot that shares a floor
with people should never move faster than a person can walk away from it.

The company has 84 employees. 61 work in Manresa, 18 in a software office in
Porto, and 5 in a small sales team in Rotterdam. Núria Balaguer is the CEO.
Ivan Prohaska is the CTO. The head of manufacturing is Aina Ferrer-Solé.

PRODUCTS
The TR-100 "Galápago" is the flagship. It is a floor transport robot that
carries up to 240 kg at a maximum speed of 1.1 metres per second. Its battery
lasts 9 hours under a typical duty cycle and charges to 80% in 35 minutes. The
TR-100 costs 41,500 euros per unit, before the service contract.

The TR-40 "Cria" is the smaller model, introduced in 2023. It carries up to
60 kg, tops out at 1.4 metres per second, and costs 17,900 euros. It is meant
for pharmacies, hospitals, and other places where the loads are light but the
corridors are narrow.

Both robots run the same control software, called Caparazón. Customers do not
pay for Caparazón separately; it is included, and updates are free for the
lifetime of the machine. Tortuga has never sold a robot without a support
contract, which costs 8% of the purchase price per year.

MANUFACTURING
Assembly happens in Manresa. The chassis is machined in Vic by a supplier
called Metal·lúrgica Riera. Battery cells come from South Korea. The company
holds roughly eleven weeks of inventory, deliberately more than the industry
norm of four, because a 2021 shortage stopped the line for two months and the
founders decided never to repeat it.

Tortuga assembled 1,340 robots in 2025: 890 TR-100 units and 450 TR-40 units.
The target for 2026 is 2,000 units.

CUSTOMERS
The largest customer is a Dutch grocery distributor that operates 210 TR-100
units across four sites. The second largest is a hospital group in Lisbon with
96 TR-40 units. No single customer accounts for more than 19% of revenue, a
limit the board set in 2022.

Revenue in 2025 was 58.4 million euros. The company has been profitable since
2024 and has never raised venture capital; it is funded by the founders, a
regional development bank, and retained earnings.

SUPPORT POLICY
A robot that fails under warranty is replaced within 48 hours anywhere in the
European Union. Outside the EU, Tortuga does not sell directly and works only
through three distributors: one in Chile, one in Morocco, and one in Japan.

The warranty is three years, extendable to seven. It does not cover water
damage, and it does not cover any robot whose safety limiter has been modified.
Modifying the limiter voids the warranty immediately and permanently — this is
the one rule the company will not negotiate on.
